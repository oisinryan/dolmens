Restoration study · Neolithic acoustic signal network

A restored portal tomb tuned for +6 dB

A fully restored dolmen, with its cairn, and a passive collector built from materials of the period, sized to give at least 6 dB of receiver gain across the 250–500 Hz signalling band.

Gain. The collector's gain is its directivity, η·4πA/λ², reduced by the curved wavefront at a cone's mouth (sinc²(δ/λ), δ the rim path excess). That is the signal-to-noise gain against diffuse background noise such as wind in vegetation. Any extra pressure gain at the throat, and the quiet of a cairn-covered chamber, would add to it but are not counted.

Status. A design study for the report's hypothesis. No collector of this kind is known from the archaeology; the stones, cairn and materials are of the period, the acoustic use is not established. Model file: restored-dolmen/dolmen-restored.glb in the repository.
